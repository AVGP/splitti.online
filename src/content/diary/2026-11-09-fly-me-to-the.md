## Rumble in the jungle

There's a bit of rumbling over in AI land. I notice, however, that LinkedIn (_insert unhealthy noises here_) is still split in two camps: Either _AI is a deus ex machina_ fixing everything and if you're not running teams of agents at all time, are you even _alive_?

Oh and the other camp that basically won't get tired to point out how the other camp _shares two brain cells, intermittently rubbing against each other_ and if you're joining them, you'll soon communicate primarily through grunting noises.

I know, I know, [after all](https://thatsaweb.site/posts/the-convincing-machine/) the things I wrote [and have done](https://aibullshit.bingo) you might think I'm in one of these camps. Probably taking a strong hint as to _which camp_ I'm in. But nope.

Nope! I think AI is a tool - and sometimes a great one, even if it's generative AI. And no, AI and generative AI are not the same thing. Far from that. But I think both have a value for a bunch of things I do regularly.

What I don't believe in is the hype.

"I haven't written code in months" - cool story, my guy. But have you actually deployed anything. Have you looked at what the systems do _inside_? Can you fix it, should the AI bot of your choice suddenly increase prices? Or will that eat up your margins? Has that software even seen serious traffic? A fault that needed troubleshooting while support requests poured in? No? Yeah, cool story, then.

In the end, _you_ are the one who's on the line. Customers enter a relationship with _you_, if you like it or not. I prefer to know how to fix shit, if and when it hits the fan, rather than having to hope for our new overlords, gen AI systems.

But if I want something to flesh out a prototype or take away the mechanical typing of boilerplate and business logic? Yes, please! I'll review that, though and - surprise, surprise - it didn't make me 100x or even 10x faster, because I have to actually research if the code does something I didn't anticipate and I have to be _very careful_ when it does, what I would've done (because I make mistakes, too, you know?).

But I give you that point for prototypes: If I wanna try something real quick, I don't have to understand the code precisely, because it might just turn out to be worthless anyway. If it isn't, that's when the work starts.

Same goes for deploying, observability, security, etc. etc. - **writing code was never the difficult part**.
