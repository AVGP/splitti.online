## Publisher Day

I learned a bunch about the news landscape in Sweden.
Turns out that people here are happy to pay for their news and they're pretty much going there directly rather than accessing news content through organic or social.
I spoke a bit about what kind of content people value on the web, got a few good questions but people seemed a bit more indifferent than in other countries I spoke in - interesting.

## Eurosky

In the last few days some issues popped up that seem to involve Eurosky and their data storage.
Sometimes I couldn't log into [popfeed.social](https://popfeed.social) where I track movies, books and series I saw, read or watched.
Other times I wasn't able to upload images or even just see the feed on [Bluesky](https://bsky.social), the social network that doesn't suck.

I used to run my own data store (in terms of the decentralised protocol called ATproto that'd be a Personal Data Server) and recently moved my data over to [Eurosky](https://eurosky.social).
That had two tempting benefits:

1. I no longer have to run my own data server and can rely on their shared infrastructure and skilled folks operating it
2. It's in Europe, not the US or other third-world countries, that's nice.

Well, (2) is still true but I'm not sure (1) holds up at the moment. I'll be monitoring the situation and might move my data back onto my own server in the future. We'll see.

## Transmission towers

I dunno why, but the built world and its infrastructure is _very interesting_ to me. So, like any normal person, I got really excited watching [this great video on transmission towers](https://youtu.be/rj-G_gmFWtE?si=resO8969JG2cXlIy).
I didn't know there's a pilot project in Switzerland that revitalises the land of a transmission line, I didn't know insulators are made from different materials these days, neither did I know that they're rewiring transmission lines with different conductors!

Cool beans!
